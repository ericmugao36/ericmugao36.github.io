*An ancient Indigenous warning, a mysterious visitor from beyond our solar system, and a question about the direction of human civilisation.*

In the deserts of Arizona, among the mesas and ancient settlements of the American Southwest, lives a people whose traditions stretch back centuries. They are the Hopi, a Native American community whose spiritual beliefs revolve around humanity's relationship with nature, the Creator and the delicate balance that sustains life.

For generations, Hopi elders have passed down teachings about the rise and fall of human civilisation. Their stories describe worlds that existed before ours, great catastrophes that destroyed them, and the possibility that humanity could once again bring about its own downfall.

But one particular story has attracted attention far beyond the Hopi homeland. It concerns nine signs that supposedly precede the end of the present world. The final sign involves a mysterious blue star appearing in the heavens.

In 2025, when astronomers observed an unusual interstellar object travelling through our solar system, some began drawing connections between the ancient warning and a visitor from another star system. The object, officially designated 3I/ATLAS, became the centre of speculation about whether an ancient prophecy was finally being fulfilled.

The story sounds extraordinary. However, to understand what is happening, we must first distinguish the traditional Hopi teachings from the modern interpretations that have grown around them.

## Nine signs before the end?

One popular account of the prophecy begins in 1958, when a Hopi elder identified as White Feather supposedly encountered a Christian minister travelling along a desert highway. During their conversation, the elder described nine signs that would appear before the end of the present world.

The first sign concerned the arrival of white-skinned people carrying weapons capable of producing thunder. The account interprets this as a reference to European settlers arriving in the Americas with firearms.

The second described spinning wheels carrying voices. This has subsequently been associated with the covered wagons used by settlers travelling west across North America.

The third spoke of strange animals resembling buffalo but possessing unusually long horns. The interpretation connects this to the introduction and expansion of longhorn cattle after the enormous buffalo populations of North America were slaughtered.

The fourth sign described iron snakes crossing the land, interpreted as the railway networks that eventually connected distant parts of the continent.

The fifth referred to a giant spiderweb covering the land. This has been associated with telegraph wires, telephone lines, electricity networks and, more recently, the internet.

The sixth described rivers of stone that would produce pictures in the sunlight. Some interpreters connect this to asphalt highways, where intense heat can produce mirages that make distant objects appear to float above the ground.

The seventh sign concerned the sea turning black and living creatures dying. This has been connected to the enormous oil spills that polluted the oceans during the twentieth century, killing birds and marine animals.

The eighth described young people with long hair seeking wisdom from Indigenous elders. The account connects this to the counterculture movement of the 1960s, when young Americans travelled to Native American communities searching for alternative spiritual teachings.

According to this version of the story, only one sign remained: the arrival of a new blue star.

It is an intriguing list, particularly because several descriptions appear to resemble developments that have transformed human civilisation. But there is an important problem with treating it as an authentic ancient prediction: the historical evidence for this particular nine-sign checklist is weak. Its appearance in published material can be traced to modern New Age literature rather than an established traditional Hopi record.

That distinction becomes important when examining the rest of the story.

## The warning that reached the United Nations

There is, however, a documented historical episode behind the broader story.

On 10 December 1992, Hopi elder and interpreter Thomas Banyacya addressed the United Nations General Assembly. He delivered a warning rooted in Hopi spiritual teachings, speaking about humanity's relationship with the natural world and the consequences of abandoning the principles that sustain life.

His message was particularly concerned with environmental destruction, increasing natural disasters and the dangers of human conflict.

Banyacya spoke of a world experiencing increasing floods, destructive storms, climate changes and earthquakes. He warned that humanity needed to change its direction or face what Hopi teachings describe as a great purification.

His appearance at the United Nations was significant. It represented an attempt to bring Indigenous perspectives into an international discussion about the future of humanity.

The circumstances surrounding the speech have since acquired an almost legendary quality. According to accounts, the following day a powerful storm struck New York, flooding roads and disrupting public transport. 

The coincidence is striking, but it does not establish that the elder predicted the storm. What matters more is the substance of his warning.

## Three worlds that came before ours

To understand the deeper meaning of the Hopi worldview, one must consider another important part of their traditional teachings: the story of the previous worlds.

In Hopi cosmology, humanity exists in the Fourth World. The preceding worlds were destroyed after humanity repeatedly abandoned the principles of cooperation, responsibility and balance.

The first world is described in traditional accounts as a time when humans and animals lived in harmony. However, people became selfish and turned against one another. The world was destroyed by fire, while those who remained faithful were saved.

The second world followed a similar pattern. Human communities prospered, but material interests and greed gradually replaced the values that had sustained them. This world ended in ice.

The third world saw humanity advance further. Communities developed, technology expanded and civilisation became increasingly sophisticated. But progress also produced arrogance, conflict and destructive warfare. Eventually, the world was destroyed by enormous floods.

The survivors entered the Fourth World, the world in which humanity supposedly lives today.

These accounts should be understood within their spiritual and cultural context rather than treated as established geological records of three previous global civilisations.

Nevertheless, their recurring theme is difficult to miss. Each world is destroyed not simply because humanity lacks knowledge or technology, but because people lose their sense of responsibility towards one another and the natural world.

The problem is not progress itself. It is progress without wisdom.

## The two paths of humanity

Perhaps the most powerful image associated with the Hopi teachings is that of two possible paths.

One represents a life lived in balance with nature, guided by humility, responsibility and spiritual awareness. The other represents a civilisation that places excessive faith in technology, material wealth and the pursuit of power.

The second path may appear impressive at first. It produces great cities, sophisticated machines, enormous economic systems and the ability to manipulate the natural environment.

But its achievements come at a cost when human beings begin to believe that technological power makes them independent of nature or superior to the principles governing life.

The Hopi concept often associated with this warning is *Koyaanisqatsi*, commonly translated as life out of balance.

It is a striking description of many of the challenges facing modern civilisation. We have developed extraordinary abilities to communicate, manufacture, travel and transform our surroundings. Yet our technological capabilities have not necessarily produced greater wisdom, cooperation or restraint.

Environmental degradation continues. Wars destroy communities. Economic inequality persists, and technological innovations often arrive faster than our ability to understand their consequences.

The question raised by these teachings is whether humanity is becoming more advanced without necessarily becoming wiser.

## The blue star and the mysterious visitor from space

The final sign in the popular nine-sign account is perhaps the most sensational.

It describes a dwelling in the heavens falling with a tremendous crash, followed by the appearance of a new blue star.

For decades, modern interpreters have attempted to associate this description with different astronomical events.

One candidate was Comet Holmes, which dramatically increased in brightness in 2007. Its expanding cloud of gas and dust became enormous, attracting considerable attention from astronomers. However, it did not appear as the blue star described in the popular account.

Then came 3I/ATLAS.

Discovered in July 2025, 3I/ATLAS was identified as the third confirmed interstellar object observed passing through our solar system. Unlike ordinary comets originating within our solar system, it came from outside our solar system.

Its unusual characteristics attracted considerable attention. Some commentators speculated about its composition, trajectory and possible artificial origins. Harvard astronomer Avi Loeb was among those who entertained the possibility that interstellar objects could warrant investigation for signs of extraterrestrial technology.

Astronomical observations identified 3I/ATLAS as a natural comet. Its blue appearance can be explained by gases released as solar heating acts on the comet, including emissions from carbon-containing molecules.

The other part of the supposed ninth sign concerns a great structure in the heavens falling to Earth. Modern interpretations have connected this to the planned retirement of the International Space Station. NASA contracted SpaceX to develop a vehicle capable of safely deorbiting the station, with its disposal planned around 2030.

The parallels are interesting, but they do not prove that the ancient Hopi anticipated either event.

## What did the Hopi actually mean?

The popular version of the prophecy presents the Hopi as people who somehow predicted railways, electricity, atomic weapons, space exploration and an interstellar comet.

But historical investigations discussed in the video raise serious doubts about that interpretation.

The nine-sign checklist appeared in modern publications decades after many of the events it supposedly predicted. The story of White Feather and the minister has also not been independently established through reliable historical documentation.

Similarly, some of the more extraordinary claims about ancient flying machines, extraterrestrial beings and hidden underground civilisations come from modern reinterpretations rather than established Hopi traditions.

In a later explanation, Thomas Banyacya's son reportedly clarified that the word *prophecy* did not fully capture what his father meant. The teachings were not necessarily a calendar of future inventions and events. They were warnings about cause and effect, particularly what happens when humanity abandons its responsibilities.

## A warning for the age of artificial intelligence

There is perhaps no better time to revisit this message than the present.

Humanity is entering an era in which artificial intelligence, biotechnology, automation and other emerging technologies are transforming nearly every aspect of society.

We are developing machines capable of producing information, analysing enormous quantities of data, influencing decisions and performing tasks that once required human intelligence.

Yet technological advancement raises questions that cannot be answered by engineering alone.

Who controls these technologies? What values guide their development? How do we prevent powerful systems from becoming instruments of exploitation, manipulation and warfare?

Artificial intelligence does not automatically make human beings more honest, compassionate or responsible. It reflects the objectives, incentives and information embedded in the systems we create.

If we build technologies around greed, deception and the pursuit of power, those same tendencies can become amplified.

The challenge, therefore, is not simply to build more powerful machines. It is to develop the wisdom and responsibility necessary to use them.

This is remarkably close to the central lesson attributed to the Hopi teachings: technology without a corresponding sense of responsibility can take humanity in a dangerous direction.
