At this point, I think my portfolio has started occupying more space in my mind than I would like to admit. I actually dreamed about completing it.

And I am not joking.

I was sleeping, but in my dream, I was still working. I was thinking about which block of code might work in a particular section, what I needed to remove, what I needed to add, and how I could make everything fit together. It was the same process I go through when I am sitting in front of my laptop.

Only this time, I was doing it in my sleep.

## It started with the Shop

I have been working on my portfolio, trying to get everything into shape. I want it to be more than a website that simply tells people who I am. I want it to showcase what I can do, the projects I have been building, and the services I offer.

The Shop section became one of the parts I really wanted to finish.

I wanted it to look right, work properly, and feel like something I could confidently show people. That meant spending time on the layout, the cards, the colours, the backgrounds, and the way everything behaves when switching between light and dark themes.

Some changes worked. Others needed more attention.

And sometimes, after making a change, I would discover that another part of the page was behaving differently from what I expected. I would go back, inspect the code, change something, test it, and then look at it again.

That is how a small change can turn into a long session.

You think you are almost done, and then you notice one more thing.

You fix it, and another thing catches your attention.

Before you know it, hours have passed.

## I even missed writing my articles

Yesterday, I did not publish any articles. Not because I had run out of ideas or because I no longer wanted to write, but because I was busy working on the portfolio.

I remember talking about the time late last night. It was around 2 a.m., and I knew I needed to go to sleep.

But going to bed and actually sleeping are two different things.

I ended up falling asleep at almost 7 a.m. and woke up around 1 p.m.

The heat did not make things any easier. During the day, I struggle to sleep properly. I might sleep for thirty minutes, wake up sweating, and try to fall asleep again. Sometimes I have very vivid dreams.

Today, one of those dreams was about finishing my portfolio.

Imagine that.

Even when I was supposed to be resting, I was still thinking about the work.

## Today was another long day

After waking up, I got back to work.

I spent some time talking with a friend, took a few breaks, and continued working on the Shop. There were moments when I was not fully productive, but even then, part of my attention was still on the website.

I would be thinking about a change I needed to make, or a block I wanted to adjust, while trying to do something else.

By the time it was approaching 1 a.m., I had spent most of the day working on the portfolio again.

The graphic design part did not take very long. I completed that in less than an hour. The Shop, however, was a different story.

It demanded much more time and attention than I had expected.

I had to keep looking at the details, testing the changes, and checking whether the page still worked as intended. There were things I wanted to improve, but I also had to be careful not to break the parts that were already working.

That balance is not always easy when you are still learning.

You want the result to look professional, but you are also learning how to make the code behave the way you want it to.

Sometimes you know what you want to achieve before you know exactly how to achieve it.

And that is where the real work begins.

## The strange thing about building something yourself

One thing I am beginning to understand is that building a website is not just about writing code.

It is also about making decisions.

Should I keep this block or remove it? Does this colour work here? Why is this section behaving differently? If I change this rule, what else might it affect?

You are constantly making small decisions, testing your assumptions, and correcting your mistakes.

And because you can always find something else to improve, it is easy to keep going long after you should have stopped.

There is always another detail.

Another adjustment.

Another idea that seems too good to leave for tomorrow.

I suppose that is part of the excitement of building something yourself. You can see what it could become, and you want to keep moving towards that vision.

But I am also learning that there has to be a point where you stop.

Not because the work is bad or because you have lost interest, but because you need to give yourself time to recover.

## Tonight, I am choosing to stop

The good news is that the Shop is working.

That matters to me because I have spent so much time trying to get it right. I still have things I want to improve, but I do not need to finish every single detail tonight.

I wanted to commit the changes to GitHub before going to sleep. I still want to do that, but I think I can leave the final check and commit for tomorrow.

I would rather return to the code with a fresh mind than keep pushing myself simply because I want to say I have finished.

There is a difference between being committed to a goal and exhausting yourself in the process of reaching it.

I am learning that difference.

I started this work because I want to build something useful, improve my skills, and have a portfolio that represents what I can do. I do not want the process of building it to become a reason I neglect everything else, including rest and writing.

So tonight, I am calling it a day.

Tomorrow, I can wake up, review what I have done, commit the changes, and continue from there.

The portfolio will still be waiting for me.

The code will still be there.

And hopefully, after some proper rest, I will be able to look at it with fresh eyes.

For now, I think that is enough.

**Sometimes, making progress means knowing when to close the laptop.**
